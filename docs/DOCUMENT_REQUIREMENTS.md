# Document Requirements by Scenario — Bill C-3

## Universal Requirements (All Applicants)

### Application Form
- **CIT 0001**: Application for a Citizenship Certificate for Adults and Minors (Proof of Citizenship) Under Section 3
- Must be opened in Adobe Acrobat Reader (not browser)
- Fill out electronically preferred, then print and sign
- If a section doesn't apply, write "NA" — never leave blank
- For minors: parent/guardian fills out as if they are the child

### Applicant Identity Documents (2 required)
- Both must show: full name AND date of birth
- At least one must have a photo
- Acceptable examples: driver's license, passport, health card, age of majority card
- For applicants outside Canada: foreign government-issued equivalents accepted (with translation if not English/French)
- **NOT accepted**: birth certificates, SIN cards, bank cards, credit cards, previous citizenship certificates

### Photographs (2 required)
- Two identical printed citizenship photos
- Must meet IRCC citizenship photo specifications (different from passport photos)
- Do NOT staple, glue, or attach directly to application
- Application will be returned if photos don't meet specifications

### Fee Payment
- $75 CAD per person
- Online payment only (credit card, Debit Mastercard, Visa Debit)
- Print TWO copies of the IRCC official receipt with barcode
- Attach one copy to application, keep one for records
- Outside Canada/US without internet: pay at Canadian embassy/consulate

### Previous Citizenship Certificates
- If replacing a certificate: must return ALL original certificates with application
- Cannot hold more than one valid certificate
- For e-certificates: delete all printed/digital copies upon submission

## Scenario-Specific Requirements

### Scenario 1: G1 — First Generation Born Abroad (Parent Born in Canada)
**Applicant's documents:**
- Long-form birth certificate (showing parents' names)
- Two pieces of ID
- Two citizenship photos
- CIT 0001 form
- Fee receipt

**Parent's documents:**
- Long-form birth certificate showing birth in Canada
- OR Canadian citizenship certificate
- OR Canadian passport (to prove citizenship)

**Complexity**: Low — often eligible for online application

---

### Scenario 2: G2 — Second Generation Born Abroad (Grandparent is Anchor)
**Applicant's documents:**
- Long-form birth certificate
- Two pieces of ID
- Two citizenship photos
- CIT 0001 form
- Fee receipt
- **Cover letter** (strongly recommended) mapping family tree

**Parent's documents:**
- Long-form birth certificate (showing grandparent as parent)
- Note: Parent does NOT need their own citizenship certificate

**Grandparent (anchor) documents:**
- Long-form birth certificate showing birth in Canada
- OR naturalization certificate/record

**If any person in chain is deceased:**
- Death certificate

**If name discrepancies exist between documents:**
- Marriage certificate(s)
- Legal name change document(s)

**Complexity**: Moderate — paper application strongly recommended

---

### Scenario 3: G3 — Third Generation Born Abroad (Great-Grandparent is Anchor)
All of Scenario 2, PLUS:

**Grandparent's documents:**
- Long-form birth certificate (showing great-grandparent as parent)

**Great-grandparent (anchor) documents:**
- Long-form birth certificate showing birth in Canada
- OR naturalization certificate/record
- Note: For pre-1900 births, church/baptismal records may be accepted as substitutes

**Complexity**: High — paper application required, legal counsel recommended

---

### Scenario 4: G4+ — Fourth+ Generation Born Abroad
All of Scenario 3, extended for each additional generation.

**Additional considerations:**
- Each additional generation requires its own long-form birth certificate
- Older records may only be available from provincial archives or church records
- Birth registration was inconsistent in many parts of Canada before the 1920s
- May need records from: Library and Archives Canada, provincial vital statistics, genealogical societies, church archives

**Complexity**: Very high — legal counsel strongly recommended

---

### Scenario 5: Prospective (Born on/after December 15, 2025)
All standard applicant documents, PLUS:

**Physical presence evidence for Canadian parent:**
The Canadian parent (if born abroad) must demonstrate 1,095 cumulative days in Canada. Acceptable proof includes:
- Employment records / T4 tax slips
- Pay stubs
- Academic transcripts / school enrollment records
- Provincial health card / immunization records
- Rental agreements / lease agreements
- Utility bills (showing Canadian address)
- Bank statements (showing Canadian transactions)
- Passport stamps (entry/exit)
- CBSA entry/exit records (check YES on consent form in application — do NOT request your own)

**Note**: Days do NOT need to be consecutive. Cumulative total of 1,095 days at any time before the child's birth/adoption.

---

### Scenario 6: Adopted Children
**Pre-December 15, 2025 adoptions:**
- May qualify for a direct grant of citizenship for adopted persons
- Use separate application: Application for Canadian Citizenship for an Adopted Person
- NOT the CIT 0001 form

**Post-December 15, 2025 adoptions:**
- Substantial connection test applies (1,095 days)
- Official adoption documents from country of adoption required

---

### Scenario 7: Crown Servants
Special exceptions for children of:
- Canadian Armed Forces members
- Federal public administration employees
- Provincial/territorial public service employees
NOT including locally engaged persons.

**Additional documents:**
- Proof of parent's/grandparent's employment in qualifying capacity
- Dates and locations of service abroad

## Document Format Requirements

### Long-Form Birth Certificates
**MUST show:**
- Child's full legal name
- Date of birth
- Place of birth
- Parents' full names

**NOT accepted:**
- Short-form birth certificates
- Computer abstracts (wallet-sized cards)
- Baptismal certificates (unless no government record exists)
- Hospital-issued birth records

### Quebec Documents
- Birth certificates and marriage certificates issued in Quebec BEFORE January 1, 1994 are NOT accepted
- Must be replaced through the Directeur de l'état civil du Québec

### Translations
Required for any document NOT in English or French:
- English or French translation
- Affidavit from translator swearing to proficiency and accuracy
- Translator must NOT be the applicant or a family member
- Canadian certified translators (provincial/territorial organization members) do NOT need an affidavit
- Affidavit must be sworn before a notary public, commissioner of oaths, or commissioner of taking affidavits

## Where to Obtain Key Documents

### Canadian Birth Certificates (by Province)
Direct users to the vital statistics office of the relevant province/territory. Each has its own process, fees, and timelines.

### US Birth Certificates
Direct users to the vital records office of the relevant US state. Most can be ordered online through VitalChek or directly from the state.

### Canadian Naturalization Records
- Library and Archives Canada may hold historical naturalization records
- IRCC may have records of more recent naturalizations

### Historical Records (Pre-1900)
- Library and Archives Canada: https://www.bac-lac.gc.ca/
- Provincial archives
- Church/parish records
- Census records (can help establish residency/birth locations)
- Immigration arrival records

## Common Pitfalls to Warn Users About

1. **Wrong birth certificate format**: Short-form certificates are the #1 reason for delays
2. **Missing documents**: Application returned if incomplete — adds months
3. **Poor quality photocopies**: Must be clear, easy-to-read COLOR copies
4. **No cover letter**: Not technically required but strongly recommended for multi-generational claims
5. **Applying online for complex cases**: Online portal wasn't designed for Bill C-3 multi-gen claims
6. **Requesting own CBSA records**: Causes delays. Let IRCC pull them via consent form.
7. **Not using Adobe Acrobat**: CIT 0001 form doesn't work properly in browser PDF viewers
8. **Leaving sections blank**: Write "NA" if not applicable — blank sections may cause returns
9. **Forgetting to sign**: Electronically filled forms still need a physical signature after printing
10. **Wrong mailing address**: Use courier address for tracked shipping, not PO Box
